Design and build a modern responsive web application called "Parts & Parcel".

Parts & Parcel is a Nigerian/African marketplace for buying and selling complete devices, machines, spare parts, components, accessories, and damaged devices for parts.

The core idea is:

"Find the machine. Find the part. Find someone who has it."

This is NOT a conventional ecommerce store.

A seller may sell:
1. A complete device/item.
2. A quantity of identical items.
3. A component/part removed from a device.
4. A damaged device together with its remaining components.
5. A damaged device whose components can later be sold individually.

A buyer can:
1. Browse complete devices.
2. Browse individual parts/components.
3. Search for a specific model or component.
4. Add listings to a cart.
5. Make offers.
6. Ask the community/forum for something they cannot find.
7. Receive responses and offers from other users.
8. Save/like listings.
9. Buy through checkout.
10. Choose how delivery will be handled.

The platform must feel trustworthy, practical, technical and marketplace-oriented rather than like a generic fashion/ecommerce website.

ITEMS AND COMPONENTS
==================================================

An ITEM represents a physical item owned by a seller.

Example:

Abel owns a damaged Dell Latitude.

Item:
Dell Latitude
Serial Number: XYZ123
Condition: Damaged

That item may have components:

- Screen
- Motherboard
- RAM
- Keyboard
- Battery
- Hard Drive

A component belongs to an item.

IMPORTANT:

An item does not necessarily have to be sold as individual components.

A seller may list:

"Damaged Dell Latitude - For Sale"

and show the components that are still present.

Alternatively, the seller can create individual listings:

"Dell Latitude Screen"
"Dell Latitude Motherboard"
"Dell Latitude RAM"

The same physical item can therefore evolve during its lifecycle.

IMPORTANT UX PRINCIPLES
==================================================

1. The listing is the main object buyers interact with.

2. Never expose database concepts.

3. A "device" can be sold as a whole or its components can be sold individually.

4. A "part" is not necessarily just an inventory SKU. It can come from a specific damaged device.

5. Quantity belongs to a listing.

6. A seller can have one listing with quantity 10.

7. A seller can have one listing with quantity 1.

8. Components should be visible when relevant.

9. Buyers should be able to browse naturally without understanding the internal inventory architecture.

10. Search should understand:
    device
    model
    component
    compatibility
    category
    brand

11. The marketplace should make it easy to discover difficult-to-find parts.

12. The Community should complement ecommerce, not feel like a completely separate product.

13. Offers should work both from listings and through community responses.

14. Delivery should be simple and explicit.

15. Never overwhelm users with technical terminology.

14. IMPORTANT TERMINOLOGY
==================================================

Use:

"Parts"

not:

"Components"

in the customer-facing navigation.

Use:

"Scrap"

as the customer-facing category/tab.

Use:

"Component"

only where it is useful inside a listing detail page.

For example:

"Components still available"

This is appropriate.

Do NOT use:

"Asset"
"Assetable"
"Polymorphic"
"Inventory Item"

anywhere in the customer UI.

==================================================
18. FINAL UX GOAL
==================================================

A user should be able to think:

"I need a Toyota Corolla."

and immediately understand:

Toyota Corolla
├── I can buy a complete Corolla
├── I can buy Corolla parts
├── I can buy a damaged Corolla for salvage
└── I can ask the community

Likewise:

"I need an HP EliteBook."

HP EliteBook
├── I can buy an EliteBook
├── I can buy EliteBook parts
├── I can buy a damaged EliteBook for salvage
└── I can ask the community

This should become one of the defining UX characteristics of Parts & Parcel.

Preserve the existing prototype's visual quality, but redesign the navigation, category pages, search experience and contextual tabs around this model.
